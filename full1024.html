Computational group theory

FULL1024210

Every group of order 1024, written down one by one.

There are 49,487,367,289 groups of order 1024. Besche, Eick and O'Brien counted them in 2001 [15], when they constructed every other group of order at most 2000 for GAP's SmallGroups library [4, 16]; David Burrell made a small correction to the count in 2022 [1]. The groups of order 1024 make up more than 99% of all groups of order at most 2000! Most of these groups had never been explicitly constructed. FULL1024 gathers all of them into a single library: each group appears exactly once, as an explicit presentation, and every family is checked against Burrell's counts (including cell sizes) [1].

The library

Two numbers sort the groups. The rank of a group G is its minimal number of generators, so that |G : Φ(G)| = 2d. The p-class is the length of the lower exponent-2 central series, which runs from G down to 1 with each term [P, G]P2 of the one before [7, 8]. The groups of p-class 2, those whose Frattini subgroup is central and elementary abelian, form cells (K, E): rank K and |Φ(G)| = 2E, with K + E = 10. A third number, the nilpotency class, is the length of the lower central series G > [G, G] > [G, G, G] > … > 1; it is at most the p-class.

Every count is checked against Burrell's [1]. For p-class 2, two independent programs that count orbits with the Cauchy–Frobenius lemma, the approach of Eick and O'Brien [10], reproduce his numbers too: the groups in cell (K, E) correspond to the orbits of GL(K, 2) on E-dimensional spaces of quadratic forms on F2K. The counts by rank and p-class outside p-class 2 come from Burrell's GRPS1024 [2] and agree with the groups in the library.

How a record works

A record K E f1 … fE lists the cell and E integers. Write the generators of G modulo Φ(G) as g1, …, gK and a basis of Φ(G) as z1, …, zE. Bit i of fl says whether zl occurs in the square gi+12. The higher bits do the same for the commutators [gi, gj], with the pairs taken in the order (1, 2), (1, 3), …, (K − 1, K).

In group terms, each fl is a quadratic form on F2K, and together they make the squaring map of G; the example below writes one out. Many records describe the same group, since a change of generators changes the forms, and the library stores exactly one record per group. In the cells of rank at most 7 it is the canonical record, so two groups there are isomorphic exactly when their canonical records agree; for the cells (8, 2) and (9, 1) it is a fixed representative. Groups of p-class 1 or at least 3 use GAP pc codes instead, the format of GAP's SmallGroups library [4, 16], and load with PcGroupCode(code, 1024).

Worked example: a record as a quadratic map

The example record of cell (6, 4), the cell that holds 96.4% of all groups, written out in full.

Take 6 4 262145 131099 74800 24884, so K = 6 and E = 4. Each fl has 21 bits, one for each monomial of degree 2 in x1, …, x6: bits 0 to 5 are the squares and bits 6 to 20 the products, with the pairs in the order (1, 2), (1, 3), …, (5, 6).

bit01234567891011121314151617181920
monomialx12x22x32x42x52x62x1x2x1x3x1x4x1x5x1x6x2x3x2x4x2x5x2x6x3x4x3x5x3x6x4x5x4x6x5x6

Writing each integer as a sum of powers of 2 and replacing each power by its monomial gives one quadratic form per zl:

integerpowers of 2quadratic form
f1 = 262145218 + 20q1 = x12 + x4x5
f2 = 131099217 + 24 + 23 + 21 + 20q2 = x12 + x22 + x42 + x52 + x3x6
f3 = 74800216 + 213 + 210 + 25 + 24q3 = x52 + x62 + x1x6 + x2x5 + x3x5
f4 = 24884214 + 213 + 28 + 25 + 24 + 22q4 = x32 + x52 + x62 + x1x4 + x2x5 + x2x6

Together they make a quadratic map q = (q1, q2, q3, q4) : F26 → F24, and it is the squaring map of G. Since every commutator is central, (ab)2 = a2b2[b, a], and so for every x ∈ F26

(g1x1 g2x2 ⋯ g6x6)2 = z1q1(x) z2q2(x) z3q3(x) z4q4(x).

The xi2 terms give the squares of the generators and the xixj terms the commutators, because the commutator map is the polarization q(x + y) + q(x) + q(y). For example x = (0, 0, 0, 1, 1, 0) gives (g4g5)2 = z1z3z4, and indeed g42 g52 [g5, g4] = z2 · z2z3z4 · z1 = z1z3z4. Read term by term, the record is this presentation, in which each z is central of order 2, so that [gi, gj] = [gj, gi]:

g1^2 = z1·z2        [g1, g4] = z4
g2^2 = z2           [g1, g6] = z3
g3^2 = z4           [g2, g5] = z3·z4
g4^2 = z2           [g2, g6] = z4
g5^2 = z2·z3·z4     [g3, g5] = z3
g6^2 = z3·z4        [g3, g6] = z2
                    [g4, g5] = z1

all other commutators are trivial
